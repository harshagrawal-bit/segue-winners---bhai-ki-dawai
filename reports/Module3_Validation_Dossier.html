READ THIS FIRST — this document was written before real-genome validation

Module 3 has since been measured against 42 real bacterial genomes with 350 laboratory-measured susceptibility results. On that test it scores 0.526 accuracy, which with 176 resistant and 174 susceptible comparisons is approximately chance. No threshold changes this; the sweep peaks at 0.503.

Every accuracy figure in this document is measured on constructed isolates — generated background sequence with real resistance genes planted into it. Those figures are correct for constructed isolates and must not be presented as real-world performance.

What did transfer is gene detection: on real genomes it found genuine determinants (mecA in 9 real S. aureus, blaSHV in 5, plus blaNDM-1, blaVIM, blaCTX-M-15) at a 13.8% false-resistance rate, though it misses 73% of laboratory-confirmed resistance because 21 reference genes cannot cover real resistance. How to read these reports

Each report compares two numbers that are not measuring the same thing, and the comparison is only meaningful once that is understood.

ColumnWhat it measuresScope
Real ICMR data Out of every sample of this bacterium tested in Indian hospitals, what share survived this antibiotic. A whole country, all samples
Module 3 output For one specific bacterial sample, does its DNA carry the instructions to survive this antibiotic. A single sample
Why the two numbers differ, and why that is correct

A national average mixes resistant and non-resistant samples together. A sample chosen because it is resistant should therefore read higher than that average. A difference is expected; its direction is what carries information, and each report states the direction expected before giving the result.

1 · Problem statement

Carbapenems are the antibiotics doctors hold back until everything else has failed. A company considering developing a new carbapenem needs to know, before committing funds, whether the bacterium it is aimed at can already survive that entire class.

Klebsiella pneumoniae is a major cause of hospital infection in India, which makes it a plausible development target and therefore a question worth answering early rather than during human trials.

Reading the difference

The 65.6% figure averages every K. pneumoniae sample tested in India, including the roughly one-third that are still treatable. The sample Module 3 examined carries blaNDM-1, so it belongs to the resistant portion. Reading above the national average is the correct outcome; reading below it would have been the error.

5 · Why we used our model here

National statistics tell a company how common resistance is. They cannot tell it why, or whether anything can be done about it. Those two questions decide whether a development programme continues.

Module 3 named the specific gene and identified its mechanism. That mechanism is the deciding detail: blaNDM-1 belongs to a category of enzyme that existing partner drugs cannot neutralise. A different gene producing an identical "carbapenem resistant" result would have been fixable.

6 · How each feature helps an R&D team

Runway — will it still work at launch

A drug starting development now reaches market around 2035. Resistance is projected to reach 84% by then, crossing the commercial viability line around 2030. The team learns the target is closing faster than the drug can be built.

Rescue — fixable or fatal

Returns DEAD END. This enzyme is not neutralised by existing partner drugs, so no reformulation saves the programme. The team re-targets rather than spending months on a fix that cannot work.

Spread — how fast it is moving

The gene sits on transferable DNA that passes between bacterial species, so the 2030 estimate should be treated as optimistic. This tightens the planning window further.

Portfolio — which candidate to back instead

Screens every candidate the team holds against every target organism at once, so the decision is not "is this one viable" but "which of ours is most viable".

7 · Conclusion

Module 3 agrees with national data on direction and magnitude, and adds three things the national data cannot supply: the gene responsible, the fact that no partner drug can rescue it, and a projection to the year the candidate would actually launch. The recommendation is to not pursue a carbapenem against this organism.

1 · Problem statement

Aminoglycosides are a family of four commonly used antibiotics. Reporting the family as a single unit is convenient but imprecise, because a bacterium can be resistant to some members and not others.

This pair was selected deliberately: it is where our model and the national data differ most, and understanding that difference is more informative than the pairs where they agree.

A limitation we identified and addressed

The 72-point difference has two separate causes, and only one of them is a fault.

Not a fault: the 27.5% is a national average across all samples, while ours was selected because it carries the gene. Our sample genuinely is amikacin-resistant.

A genuine fault: declaring the whole family resistant was too broad. Two reference databases — CARD and NCBI — both confirm this gene does not affect gentamicin. Reporting the family as lost discarded a drug that still works.

Module 3 now reports at individual-drug level for this gene, so gentamicin is correctly identified as remaining available.

5 · Why we used our model here

The national figure records how often amikacin fails. It does not indicate which other drugs in the same family remain usable, because the gene responsible is not identified.

Module 3 identifies the gene and consults reference databases for the individual drugs that gene affects. The practical result is that one antibiotic is recovered from a family that would otherwise have been written off entirely.

6 · How each feature helps an R&D team

Rescue — with individual-drug detail

Returns SALVAGEABLE and names gentamicin as unaffected. The family is not lost, and the team retains a development option it would otherwise have discarded.

Runway — a slower decline

Resistance is rising about 2.2 points per year, projected to 47% by 2035 — below the viability line. Unlike Report 01, this target remains commercially addressable.

Portfolio — comparing across the pipeline

Places this partial resistance beside the team's other candidates, so a usable-but-narrowed option can be weighed against fully clear ones.

Unknown-element check

Confirms every foreign section of this DNA is accounted for by an identified gene, so no unexplained resistance is present.

7 · Conclusion

This pair demonstrates both a limitation and its correction. Comparing against real national data revealed that family-level reporting was too coarse; the module now resolves to individual drugs where the reference data supports it. The practical outcome is that gentamicin is correctly retained as a viable option for this sample.

1 · Problem statement

The previous two reports both involved enzymes — the bacterium producing something that attacks or alters the drug. This pair uses a different mechanism entirely, which matters because the available responses differ.

A sample carrying the mecA gene is what is commonly called MRSA. The gene supplies the bacterium with a replacement component that penicillin-type antibiotics cannot attach to.

Reading the difference

The 52.4% states what proportion of all Indian S. aureus samples are MRSA. Our sample is an MRSA sample — it carries mecA. The two numbers therefore answer different questions: one is how common the category is, the other is whether this particular sample belongs to it.

A high reading is the only correct outcome here. Anything near 52% would have indicated the module had failed to recognise a gene it detected at 98% confidence.

5 · Why we used our model here

This pair was included to demonstrate that the module distinguishes between mechanisms rather than treating all resistance identically.

In Report 01 the bacterium destroys the drug; in Report 02 it alters the drug. In both cases a partner drug is at least conceivable. Here the bacterium has replaced the component the drug needs to attach to, so there is nothing for a partner drug to act on. Module 3 reaches DEAD END by a different route, and gives the correct reason.

6 · How each feature helps an R&D team

Rescue — the reason, not just the verdict

States that no partner drug applies because there is no enzyme to block. A team that assumed the enzyme-blocking approach from Report 01 would transfer here is corrected immediately.

Runway — the steepest trend in this dossier

Rising about 2.8 points per year, reaching 75% by 2035. Of the three pairs, this target is deteriorating fastest.

Spread — a slower transfer mechanism

This gene travels on an element embedded in the chromosome rather than a free-floating one, so it spreads more slowly. Module 3 applies a smaller acceleration factor to the projection and states why.

Value — the cost of finding out later

Converts the finding into the figure a budget committee needs: what carrying this programme to trial stage would have cost before the same result emerged. All inputs are adjustable.

7 · Conclusion

Module 3 correctly identified a resistance mechanism that works differently from the other two pairs and reported the appropriate consequence. Taken together, the three reports show the module distinguishing enzyme-based resistance that can be worked around, enzyme-based resistance that cannot, and replacement-based resistance where the question does not arise.

What the three pairs establish

  • Three mechanisms, correctly distinguished. The module does not treat resistance as a single phenomenon — it identifies how each gene works and gives the matching recommendation.
  • Two different verdicts from similar-looking results. Reports 01 and 02 both begin as enzyme-based resistance and end in opposite recommendations.
  • One limitation found and corrected. Report 02's difference against real data exposed family-level reporting as too coarse. The correction recovered a usable antibiotic.
  • Direction is consistent throughout. In all three pairs the module reads above the national average, which is the expected outcome when examining samples selected for carrying resistance genes.

Stated limitations

Declared for completeness

Gene sequences are real; surrounding DNA is generated. All 21 reference genes come from NCBI's published database with verifiable accession numbers. The remaining sample DNA is constructed, because complete annotated genomes exceed what a demonstration can carry.

Sample contents are assigned by us, not laboratory-confirmed. We select which genes each sample carries, so these are not independent laboratory results.

Held-out score is 0.763, not higher. Replacing generated gene sequences with real ones reduced measured accuracy from 0.99 to 0.92, and to 0.763 on the most demanding test. The reduction was retained because it reflects genuine performance.
