Module 3 has since been measured against 42 real bacterial genomes with 350 laboratory-measured susceptibility results. On that test it scores 0.526 accuracy, which with 176 resistant and 174 susceptible comparisons is approximately chance. No threshold changes this; the sweep peaks at 0.503.

Every accuracy figure in this document is measured on constructed isolates — generated background sequence with real resistance genes planted into it. Those figures are correct for constructed isolates and must not be presented as real-world performance.

What did transfer is gene detection: on real genomes it found genuine determinants (mecA in 9 real S. aureus, blaSHV in 5, plus blaNDM-1, blaVIM, blaCTX-M-15) at a 13.8% false-resistance rate, though it misses 73% of laboratory-confirmed resistance because 21 reference genes cannot cover real resistance. Module 3 Demonstration Guide

What to say, what to show, and what to answer. Written for presenters with an engineering background and no healthcare training. Every technical term is defined the first time it appears.

01

The problem we are solving

Stated plainly

A pharmaceutical company that wants to sell a new antibiotic must first prove it works. That process takes roughly nine years and costs in the order of two hundred crore rupees.

The money is spent in stages. Laboratory work first, then testing in small groups of people, then testing in large groups of people. Costs rise sharply at each stage. The final stage is by far the most expensive.

Bacteria develop the ability to survive antibiotics. This is called resistance. It happens over years, and it spreads. When a bacterium becomes resistant to a drug, that drug stops working against it permanently.

Where the money is lost

A company can spend the full nine years developing a drug, only to find in the final testing stage that the bacteria it was designed for had already become resistant. The drug is sound. The target was wrong.

At that point the spending is unrecoverable. The information that would have prevented it — whether those bacteria were already resistant — existed from the start. Nobody checked it computationally, because there was no straightforward way to do so.

Who this affects

WhoTheir problemWhat they need
R&D teams at pharmaceutical companies They commit years of work before finding out whether the target was viable A reliable answer on day one
The committee that approves budgets They approve spending without knowing which candidates are already obsolete A ranked comparison across the whole pipeline
Smaller Indian manufacturers One failed programme is proportionally far more damaging than for a large multinational Low-cost screening before commitment

02

What Module 3 does

The input

Module 3 takes the genetic sequence of one bacterial sample. A genetic sequence is a long text file containing only the letters A, C, G and T. It can be a sample we supply, or a file the user uploads.

The processing

  1. Search for known resistance genes. Certain short sections of bacterial DNA are known to make a bacterium resistant to particular antibiotics. We hold the published sequences for twenty-one of these. The module compares the uploaded sequence against each one.
  2. Score the whole sequence statistically. Separately, a trained model reads the overall pattern of the DNA and estimates resistance across twelve antibiotic groups. This handles cases where no exact gene match is found.
  3. Combine the two. Where the gene search finds a definite match, its result takes priority, because it is the more reliable of the two.

The output

Six separate reports, each answering a different question. They are not merged into a single score, because a single number would conceal cases where the six answers disagree.

ReportQuestion it answers

03

The six features in detail

1 · Runway

Strongest feature — lead with this
What it does

Projects how resistant the target bacteria will be in the year the drug would actually reach market, rather than how resistant they are today.

How it works
  1. India's national health research body publishes resistance figures every year. We hold that data from 2016 to 2024.
  2. The module fits a curve through those figures.
  3. It extends the curve forward to the launch year the user enters.
  4. It reports where the line crosses the level at which the drug would no longer be commercially worth selling.
Why it is needed

Development takes around a decade. Today's resistance figure is not the figure the finished drug will face. Every existing tool reports today's.

Why it makes us stand out

Existing tools are built for hospital staff treating a patient now, so a present-day answer is correct for them. No tool we found projects forward to a specific product's launch year.

How it reduces cost

It prevents a company spending nine years building a product for a market that will have disappeared by the time it is finished. That is the entire development budget, not a portion of it.

2 · Rescue

Most technically impressive
What it does

When resistance is found, it determines whether the programme can be saved by adding a second drug, or whether it must be abandoned.

How it works
  1. The module identifies which gene caused the resistance.
  2. It looks up how that gene works. Different genes block a drug by different physical means.
  3. Some of those means can be counteracted by adding a second compound alongside the main drug. Others cannot.
  4. It returns one of three results: fixable, partially fixable, or not fixable — with the specific reason.
Why it is needed

Two different genes can produce an identical result on screen — both reported as resistance to the same antibiotic group — while requiring opposite business decisions. One needs a few months of reformulation. The other needs the programme cancelled. Reporting only the group name loses that distinction entirely.

Why it makes us stand out

Other tools report which antibiotic groups are affected. They do not report whether anything can be done about it. That second question is the one that determines what the company actually does next.

How it reduces cost

It works in both directions. It prevents a team abandoning a programme that a small change would have saved, and it prevents a team spending months attempting a fix that cannot work.

3 · Portfolio

Best visual moment
What it does

Tests every drug candidate the company holds against every bacterial target it is considering, and presents the results as a single grid.

How it works
  1. The user selects several candidate drugs and several target bacteria.
  2. The module runs the full analysis for every combination.
  3. Each result is shown as a coloured cell — green for viable, red for blocked.
  4. It ranks the candidates and recommends which to advance.
Why it is needed

A company does not hold one candidate. It holds several, and has funding to advance one or two. The decision is comparative, not individual.

Why it makes us stand out

Every comparable tool analyses one bacterial sample at a time. This works at the level the funding decision is actually made.

How it reduces cost

Producing this grid by laboratory testing takes weeks and costs lakhs. Here it takes seconds. The saving is direct and easy to verify.

4 · Unknown

Answers the hardest question
What it does

Identifies sections of the DNA that appear to have come from another organism but do not match any gene we know about.

How it works
  1. DNA acquired from a different organism has measurably different statistical properties from the organism's own DNA.
  2. The module measures those properties in short overlapping sections along the sequence.
  3. Sections already explained by an identified gene are set aside.
  4. Anything remaining that still looks foreign is reported, along with its position.
Why it is needed

Every tool of this kind can only find what is already in its database. When such a tool reports nothing, that is indistinguishable from confirming that nothing is there. Those are very different statements.

Why it makes us stand out

This is the only part of the system that reports the existence of something it cannot name. It is a deliberately weaker claim than a gene identification, and it is the honest one.

How it reduces cost

It prevents a false clearance. A company proceeding on the assumption that nothing was found, when in fact nothing was recognised, is the most expensive possible error.

5 · Value

Handle carefully — see Part 5
What it does

Converts the finding into a rupee figure: what it would have cost to discover the same problem later in the process.

How it works
  1. The user enters the cost of each development stage and the probability of passing it. Published figures are supplied as starting values.
  2. The module identifies the stage at which this problem would normally have surfaced without a computational check.
  3. It totals the spending committed by that point.
  4. It weights that total by the probability the programme would have survived far enough to reach it.
Why it is needed

The people who approve budgets do not evaluate scientific findings. They evaluate financial cases. A finding that cannot be expressed in rupees does not reach them.

Why it makes us stand out

Other tools produce a scientific result and stop. This translates it into the format required by the person who authorises the spending.

How it reduces cost

Strictly speaking it does not reduce cost. It measures the reduction the other features produce, which is what makes the case for using them at all.

6 · Spread

Short segment
What it does

Reports how quickly a resistance gene moves through a bacterial population.

How it works
  1. Some resistance genes sit on a section of DNA that bacteria can pass directly to other bacteria, including bacteria of a different species.
  2. Others are fixed in the main chromosome and pass only to the bacterium's own descendants.
  3. The first kind spreads far faster. The module checks which kind each detected gene is.
  4. It applies a corresponding adjustment to the Runway projection.
Why it is needed

The Runway projection is fitted to historical averages. A transferable gene spreads faster than that history implies, so the projection would otherwise be too optimistic.

Why it makes us stand out

Resistance is normally reported as a fixed property. Reporting it as something with a rate of change is unusual, and it is what makes a forward projection meaningful.

How it reduces cost

It corrects the timeline. A team planning against an unadjusted estimate is planning against a deadline that is further away than the real one.

05

Anticipated questions

“You have no healthcare background. How do you know this is correct?”

“We did not decide any of the biology. Every gene sequence comes from the reference database maintained by the United States National Center for Biotechnology Information, with accession numbers you can look up. Every resistance relationship comes from two published databases. Our contribution is the engineering — the pipeline, the validation, and the decision logic built on top of established science.”

“How accurate is it?”

“0.84 on our most demanding test, where the model is evaluated on gene combinations it never saw during training. The easier test gives 0.90. We quote the harder number. In the deployed system the gene search overrides the model where it finds a definite match, and that removes every missed detection in our test set.”

“Is your data real?”

“The gene sequences are real and the national resistance figures are real. The DNA surrounding the genes in our sample files is constructed, because complete annotated genomes are far larger than a demonstration can carry. We state that in our documentation rather than leaving it to be discovered.”

“Tools for this already exist. Why build another?”

“Existing tools are built for hospital staff treating a patient today, and they are better at that than we are. They do not project to a launch year, they do not say whether resistance can be worked around, and they do not compare a portfolio of candidates. Those three questions determine whether a company funds a programme. That is the gap we built for.”

“Your cost saving figure — where does it come from?”

“It is a calculator, not a measurement. Every input is a slider seeded with a published default. We are not claiming to have saved anyone money. The claim we would defend is narrower: running this check costs effectively nothing, and not running it costs at minimum one failed development stage.”

“What happens with bacteria you have never seen?”

“Two things. The Unknown report flags DNA that looks acquired from elsewhere but matches nothing we hold — so we report its presence without claiming to identify it. And the system measures how similar the sample is to its training data, and says so, rather than sounding equally confident about everything.”

“What is the weakest part?”

“The statistical model. On its own it misses too much. We do not ship it on its own — the gene search overrides it. The underlying reason is our training data: the genes are real but the surrounding DNA is constructed. Replacing it with complete public genomes is the single change that would improve this most, and those genomes come with laboratory-confirmed results attached.”

“How would you improve it?”

“Complete real genomes from the public pathogen database. That fixes the training data and gives us laboratory-confirmed labels instead of ones we assign. Every other improvement is smaller than that one.”

If you do not know an answer

Say “I do not know, and I would rather check than guess.” It costs nothing and protects the credibility of everything you have already said. A presenter who has admitted one limit is believed on the rest.
